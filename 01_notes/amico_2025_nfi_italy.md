## Title and Authors of the Paper

*National Forest Inventory in Italy: new perspectives for forest monitoring* — D'Amico, Chirici, Campetella et al. (2025), Opinion Paper, Open Access. Published online October 2025, affiliated with geoLAB – University of Florence and CREA Research Centre for Forestry and Wood.

## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Climate change and increasing disturbance regimes are intensifying the information needs of forest policy and management, making traditional periodic inventories insufficient to capture rapidly changing forest conditions.
- **What was done?** The authors outline the innovations introduced in the new Italian National Forest Inventory (IFNI), including a transition to annual estimates, enhanced remote sensing integration, stakeholder co-design, and alignment with biodiversity monitoring frameworks.
- **What is the main outcome?** The new IFNI provides a modernised framework for continuous, high-resolution national forest monitoring that can inform sustainable forest management and climate policy while serving as a model for other national and international inventory efforts.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

The paper argues that the traditional paradigm of periodic national forest inventories (NFIs) is no longer adequate given the accelerating pace of climate-driven forest disturbance (wildfires, windstorms, bark beetle outbreaks, drought). The central goal of the new Italian NFI is to transition to annual, continuous forest monitoring — capturing not only standing forest attributes but also rates and types of change — while integrating remote sensing technologies and aligning with international frameworks such as ICP Forests. The overarching concept is an "enhanced NFI" that combines in-situ plot measurements with wall-to-wall remote sensing products.

## Technical Approach

The new IFNI adopts tessellation stratified sampling as its probabilistic design framework, enabling spatially balanced, statistically rigorous plot placement across the national territory. The inventory transitions from periodic to annual estimates using a rolling panel design in which a subset of plots is remeasured each year, ensuring annual change detection while maintaining national representativeness. Forest attributes measured span multiple scales: tree-level data (total height, crown base height, canopy projection, tree-related microhabitats, increment cores), plot-level data (species composition, vertical structure, diameter distributions, biomass, dead wood), and landscape-level integration through remote sensing (Sentinel-2, LiDAR, IRIDE satellite constellation). The inventory is partially integrated with the ICP Forests monitoring programme, and new dedicated surveys address biodiversity components (tree microhabitats, understorey vegetation). Data from the new IFNI will be made publicly available at tree and plot levels, with plot coordinates released for scientific use subject to a non-disclosure agreement.

## Distinctive Features

The IFNI introduces three especially novel features: (1) a fully participatory design process in which stakeholder needs drove methodological choices before field data collection; (2) the operational integration of wall-to-wall remote sensing mapping (including existing Italian products for forest biomass, area, and disturbance) as an integral component of the inventory, creating a hybrid field–satellite monitoring system; and (3) the explicit targeting of large-scale disturbances (VAIA windstorm damage, wildfire, bark beetle, drought-induced mortality) through annual change detection — a shift from characterising stable forest states to tracking dynamic ecosystem change.

## Experimental Setup and Results

As an opinion paper, the study does not present original experimental results but synthesises the design, rationale, and expected outputs of the new IFNI. It draws on the experience of the two previous Italian NFIs (IFN85 and INFC2005) and contextualises Italian developments within the broader European NFI landscape (ENFIN, COST harmonisation initiatives). Key innovations are illustrated with reference to existing Italian forest monitoring products: wall-to-wall biomass and volume increment mapping (Giannetti et al. 2022), VAIA windstorm damage mapping using Sentinel-2 (Giannetti et al. 2021), and integration with the Glasgow Forest Declaration data needs. The paper also discusses how annual estimates support policy reporting obligations (LULUCF regulation, EU Biodiversity Strategy, Forest Strategy).

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages:** Annual estimates dramatically improve the detection of rapid changes due to disturbances, filling a critical gap left by periodic inventories with cycles of 10–15 years. The participatory approach ensures that inventory outputs directly address the needs of the primary users. Enhanced integration with remote sensing reduces the cost per unit of information and enables spatial extrapolation of plot-based estimates.

**Limitations:** Transitioning from periodic to annual inventories substantially increases logistical and financial demands. The statistical properties of rolling panel designs (including handling of panel imbalance and model-assisted estimation) are complex and require methodological investment. Release of plot coordinates, even under non-disclosure agreements, raises concerns about privacy and forest security (e.g., facilitating illegal logging). Integration of heterogeneous remote sensing products (varying sensors, resolutions, and acquisition dates) introduces additional harmonisation challenges.

## Conclusion

The new Italian National Forest Inventory represents a significant modernisation of national forest monitoring, driven by the recognition that climate change has fundamentally altered the temporal dynamics of forest ecosystems. By combining annual field remeasurement with remote sensing integration, stakeholder co-design, and biodiversity-targeted surveys, IFNI offers a blueprint for next-generation NFIs that can support both science and policy in a period of rapid forest change. The innovations discussed are directly relevant to the broader European and global effort to modernise forest inventory systems.
